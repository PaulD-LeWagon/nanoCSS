## The two main architectural approaches are:

- Template-switching — the app detects or asks the project type and serves a completely different template set. Simple to build, but you end up maintaining multiple parallel systems.
- Abstraction — you find the universal bones that all plans share (Goal → Milestones → Tasks → Review) and build around those, with project-type-specific "skins" or field sets on top. Harder to design, but more coherent as a product.

My instinct is that approach 2 is the right long-term answer, but approach 1 is the right MVP. 
Start with IT projects, ship it, then figure out the abstraction when you have real users using it for different things.

The meta-irony here — and how to use it

You're using an agentic dev studio methodology to build an agentic dev studio tool. That's actually a significant advantage: you are your own primary user. Every friction point you hit while using your own templates to build this product is direct product feedback. Keep a running log of those moments — they'll be your best source of feature ideas.

What "project creation" actually means

There's a spectrum here worth thinking about:

- A document generator — you answer questions and it produces filled-in templates (relatively simple)
- A project workspace — you manage the living project inside the app, not just generate docs (much more complex)
- A guided workflow — the app walks you through each phase in order, prompting the right questions at the right time (the most interesting and differentiated option)

The third option maps most directly to what your methodology is actually doing — it's a wizard that takes you from fuzzy concept to agent-ready brief. That's a genuine product, not just a document generator with a UI.

Who is the user of this app other than you? Because "other indie devs using this methodology" is a very small and specific market, whereas "anyone who needs to take a project from idea to plan" is enormous but vague. 
The answer shapes everything — the UI, the pricing, the marketing, even which template types to build first.
